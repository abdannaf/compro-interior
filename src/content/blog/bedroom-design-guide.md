## The Art of Bedroom Layout Design

Your bedroom is more than just a place to sleep — it's your personal sanctuary where you begin and end each day. Getting the layout right is essential for creating a space that promotes rest, relaxation, and rejuvenation.

In this guide, we'll walk you through the fundamental principles of bedroom layout design that our team at BuildPRO uses for every residential project.

## Understanding Traffic Flow

The first step in any bedroom layout is mapping the traffic flow. You need clear pathways between:

- **The door and the bed** — at least 90cm wide
- **The bed and the wardrobe** — enough space to open doors fully
- **The bed and the bathroom** — especially important for master bedrooms

A well-planned traffic flow ensures you can move comfortably through the space even in the dark.

## The Bed Placement Rule

The bed is the focal point of any bedroom. Here are our placement guidelines:

1. **Center the headboard on the longest wall** when possible
2. **Allow at least 60cm on each side** for nightstands and movement
3. **Avoid placing the bed directly under a window** — drafts affect sleep quality
4. **Keep the bed away from the door's swing path** for safety and comfort

## Zoning Your Bedroom

Modern bedrooms often serve multiple purposes. Create distinct zones for:

- **Sleep zone** — the bed, nightstands, and ambient lighting
- **Dressing zone** — wardrobe, mirror, and preparation area
- **Work/reading zone** — desk, reading chair, or window seat
- **Storage zone** — organized closet systems and hidden storage

## Lighting Layers

Effective bedroom lighting uses three layers:

**Ambient lighting** provides overall illumination. Consider recessed ceiling lights or a central pendant that can be dimmed.

**Task lighting** serves specific functions — bedside lamps for reading, vanity lights for grooming, and desk lamps for work.

**Accent lighting** adds atmosphere — LED strips behind the headboard, wall sconces, or decorative table lamps.

## Furniture Proportions

One of the most common mistakes in bedroom design is choosing furniture that's either too large or too small for the space:

- In rooms under 12m², stick to a queen-size bed maximum
- King-size beds need at least 16m² to feel proportional
- Keep nightstands proportional to bed height
- A dresser should not block window light

## Storage Integration

Smart storage solutions keep bedrooms clutter-free:

- **Built-in wardrobes** maximize wall space
- **Under-bed storage** utilizes dead space
- **Floating shelves** display items without floor footprint
- **Ottoman beds** with hydraulic lift mechanisms offer hidden storage
